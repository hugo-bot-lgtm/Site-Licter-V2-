Language coverage is not translation

A tool that reports twenty languages is telling you about collection, not about understanding. The gap between the two is where most multi-market reads go wrong.

Licter analysis team30 July 20262 min read

Cet article n’est disponible qu’en anglais. Le reste du site bascule en français.

IN THIS PIECE

  1. Three different claims
  2. Where it breaks
  3. A cheap check

Three different claims

“We collect in twenty languages” means the crawler does not choke on the character set. “We classify in twenty languages” means sentiment and topic models exist for each. “We understand twenty languages” means somebody who speaks them has looked at the output. Vendors are usually making the first claim in the vocabulary of the third.

The distinction is not pedantic. Collection without classification gives you volume charts you cannot read. Classification without review gives you confident charts that are wrong in ways you cannot see.

The chart will show those markets as quiet. They are not quiet; they are invisible.

Where it breaks

Irony and negation are the classic failures, and they are unevenly distributed across languages. So are brand names that are also common words, which turn one market’s perimeter into noise while the neighbouring market stays clean.

The subtler failure is category vocabulary. The words a market uses for a product benefit rarely map one to one, and a taxonomy built in the headquarters language will quietly under-count the markets that phrase things differently. The chart will show those markets as quiet. They are not quiet; they are invisible.

A cheap check

Take two hundred items per market, have a native speaker classify them by hand, and compare against the machine. It takes a day per market and it tells you exactly which markets you can report on and which ones need a caveat.

Nobody enjoys doing this. Every team that has done it has changed at least one conclusion.

What to take away

  • Collection, classification and understanding are three different claims.
  • Irony, negation and ambiguous brand names fail unevenly by language.
  • A taxonomy built in one language under-counts markets that phrase things differently.
  • Two hundred hand-coded items per market will tell you what you can report on.
