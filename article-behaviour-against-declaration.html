Behaviour against declaration

The strongest consumer insight is usually the one where somebody’s stated preference and their observable behaviour come apart. Finding those gaps is a method, not luck.

Licter analysis team6 August 20262 min read

Start from the contradiction, not the topic

Most insight briefs start from a subject: what do people think about our category. The output is a description, and descriptions rarely change decisions.

A more productive starting point is a contradiction you already suspect. Customers say price is the reason they leave; retention data says they leave after a service failure. Now the conversation data has a job: which of those two shows up when people talk unprompted, and in what proportion?

People talk to brands differently than they talk about them.

Where to look for the gap

Reviews and complaint threads are where declaration collapses, because the person is motivated and unobserved. Comparison and recommendation threads — someone asking a community what to buy — are where the real decision criteria surface, because the stakes are personal and the audience is peers.

Advertising and brand accounts are the worst place to look. The conversation there is with the brand, and people talk to brands differently than they talk about them.

The output that survives the meeting

A gap finding is only actionable if it names the decision it changes. “People say price, they discuss service” is interesting. “People say price, they discuss service, and the service complaints cluster in the two weeks after delivery” is a brief for an operations team.

Specificity about when and where is what turns an insight into a piece of work somebody can be assigned.

What to take away

  • Start from a suspected contradiction, not a topic.
  • Reviews and peer recommendation threads carry real criteria.
  • Brand-owned channels are the worst place to look for them.
  • Name the decision the gap changes, with a when and a where.
