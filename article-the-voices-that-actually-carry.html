The voices that actually carry

Follower count measures how many people could see something. It says almost nothing about whether anything moves when they do.

Audience size is a ceiling, not a force

A large account tells you the maximum number of impressions available. Whether those impressions turn into conversation, and whether that conversation reaches anyone new, are separate questions that size does not answer.

The accounts that move a category are frequently mid-sized and specific. They sit inside a community rather than above it, and what they say gets repeated in the community’s own words.

Three things worth measuring instead

Onward conversation. Not shares — shares are cheap. New threads started by other people, referencing the idea without linking the source.

Community reach beyond their own. Does the topic appear in communities where that account is not present? That is the difference between an audience and a bridge.

Vocabulary adoption. If the framing survives — if people start using the words that account introduced — something real happened. If the framing dies with the post, you bought impressions.

The uncomfortable consequence

Measured this way, some very expensive partnerships stop looking like partnerships and start looking like media buys. That is a legitimate thing to buy, but it should be bought under its own name and evaluated with media metrics.

The corollary is that the accounts that do carry are often not on anybody’s list, are not looking for a brief, and cost a fraction of the ones that are.

What to take away

  • Follower count is a ceiling on impressions, not evidence of movement.
  • Measure onward threads, not shares.
  • Reach into communities the account is not part of is the real signal.
  • If the framing dies with the post, you bought media — price it as media.
