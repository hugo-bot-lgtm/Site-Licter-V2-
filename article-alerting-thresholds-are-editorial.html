Alerting thresholds are an editorial choice

A threshold looks like a technical setting. It is really a statement about what your organisation considers worth waking someone up for — and it should be argued about in those terms.

19 August 20262 min readLicter analysis team

The number is the least interesting part

Set an alert at three hundred mentions an hour and you will be told about football. Set it at thirty and you will be told about everything. Teams spend weeks tuning that number and very little time on the question underneath it: what are we actually trying not to miss?

Two organisations with identical volumes can be right to set wildly different thresholds, because they are exposed to different things. A retailer fears a product safety claim spreading in a parenting community at low volume. A public body fears a mid-volume rumour with political pickup. Those are not the same alert.

Twenty mentions in a market where you normally see fifteen a week is the entire story.

Threshold per topic, per market

A single global threshold is a guarantee of two failures at once: noise in your largest market and silence in your smallest. Twenty mentions in a market where you normally see two hundred a day is nothing. Twenty mentions in a market where you normally see fifteen a week is the entire story.

Per-topic matters as much. Recruitment chatter and product failure chatter should not share a trigger, because the cost of missing them is not comparable.

Write down the cost of a miss

The clean way to settle threshold arguments is to make the trade explicit. For each topic, write one line: what happens if we hear about this six hours late. Some answers are “nothing”. Some are not. The thresholds fall out of the list almost by themselves, and — more usefully — the list is reviewable by people who do not read dashboards.

It also gives you something to point at when someone asks why they were paged at two in the morning.

What to take away

  • Thresholds encode what you are willing to miss.
  • One global threshold guarantees noise in big markets and silence in small ones.
  • Set them per topic and per market.
  • Write the cost of a six-hour delay for each topic, and let the numbers follow.
