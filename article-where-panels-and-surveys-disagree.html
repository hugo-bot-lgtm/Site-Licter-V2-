Where panels and surveys disagree

When observed behaviour and declared intention point in opposite directions, the useful move is not to pick a winner. It is to work out which question each one actually answered.

16 September 20262 min readLicter analysis team

They are not measuring the same thing

A survey asks what someone will do, in a context where they know they are being asked. Social data records what they said unprompted, to an audience they chose, for reasons that have nothing to do with your study. Neither is the truth; they are two different instruments pointed at two different moments.

Disagreement between them is therefore not a failure. It is information about the gap between how people present a decision and how they discuss it.

The usual shapes of disagreement

Declared higher than observed. Typical of anything with a social desirability attached — sustainability, health, thrift. People mean it when they say it and do not do it.

Observed higher than declared. Typical of anything slightly embarrassing, or simply too mundane to mention in a survey. Categories people use constantly and never think about.

Different segments entirely. The most interesting case: both instruments are right, about different people. The survey sample and the conversation population do not overlap as much as either side assumes.

Reconcile before you report

The reconciliation is worth doing explicitly and worth showing. Put the two numbers side by side, name the gap, and say what you think produces it. That single slide does more for the credibility of an insights function than any individual finding.

The alternative — quietly dropping whichever instrument disagreed with the recommendation — is how insight teams lose the room, usually about eighteen months later.

What to take away

  • Surveys measure prompted intention; social data records unprompted behaviour.
  • Social desirability inflates declaration; mundanity deflates it.
  • Sometimes both are right about different populations.
  • Show the reconciliation instead of dropping the inconvenient instrument.
