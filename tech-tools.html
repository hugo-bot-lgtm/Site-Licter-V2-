One question, four ways of hearing it.

Each layer answers something the others cannot. Used alone, any one of them gives a confident and partial answer, which is the most expensive kind.

The question“Our sales are flat among under-30s. Why?”

What it hears

Under-30s talk about the brand, but mostly to say it is “for their parents”. The word comes back in one post in five.

ReadsVolumes, topics, sentiment, how a conversation travels

Cannot tell youWho these people are beyond their post

What it hears

The under-30s who mention the brand follow food creators and sustainability accounts, not the brand's own channels.

ReadsInterests, passions, brand affinities, media relationships

Cannot tell youWhat they buy

What it hears

They do buy the category, twice as often as their parents, but from two competitors with smaller formats.

ReadsBehaviour of about three billion consumers, nobody recruited

Cannot tell youWhat they think of you

What it hears

Their searches combine the category with “small”, “single portion” and “recyclable”. Your range answers none of the three.

ReadsGoogle and Amazon queries, before the purchase and after the problem

Cannot tell youHow loud the topic is in public

Our read, all four together

It is not an image problem, it is a format problem: launch a single-portion, recyclable range, and speak through the creators they already follow.
